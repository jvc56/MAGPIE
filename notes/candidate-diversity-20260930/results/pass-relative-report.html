Pass-relative candidate diversity rerun

Setup finds useful additional plays at the 15-second selection budget. The benefit is concentrated in late positions. Its advantage over an exactly same-size static pool remains uncertain in this sample; a separately frozen 1,200-game confirmation study tests that comparison overnight.

Explore all candidates, sortable sim results and pass/play adjustments

The corrected rerun includes 256 occupied-board positions, 64 openings, width and same-count controls on 128 positions, six-ply controls on 64, doubled check samples on 128, 40 exchange/tile-quota positions, and independent 10-minute sixteen-ply validations at all 14 primary positions where a policy selects outside static25, plus two quota examples. Rollouts use no-PAT static play. Every distinct selection pool receives 15 seconds.

Blocking adds 1.4 × the reduction in the opponent’s best placement score versus pass. Setup adds 0.75 × the improvement in our next-turn best placement score after the opponent reply versus the matched pass/reply branch. Setup branches share the candidate leave and conditioned refill; they isolate board opportunity. Terminal opponent replies give zero next-turn opportunity. Exchanges have zero board adjustments.

Subtracting the shared pass reply baseline changes the interpretation of blocking values but preserves its candidate ranking. Setup admission changes because its matched baseline depends on candidate leave/refill. The main checks average 64 paired opponent racks; doubled-sample controls measure admission stability.
